Autumn

The long way round

There is a footpath behind the old mill that nobody seems to use any more. It follows the river for a mile, crosses a stone bridge, and then climbs gently through beech woods until the town disappears behind you.

I walk it most Sundays. The point is not to get anywhere in particular; the point is the hour in between, when the only decisions are which side of a puddle to take and whether the light is worth stopping for.

Late summer

Notes on a slow kettle

The kettle in the cabin takes nine minutes to boil. I used to find this infuriating. Now it is the part of the morning I would least like to lose.

Written by hand, served plainly. No cookies, no tracking.
